Chapter 1: Perspectives on Insurance & Risk Management

1.1 History of Insurance

Insurance originated in ancient China and Babylon (Code of Hammurabi), developing through medieval marine contracts in Genoa and Venice, and expanding after the Great Fire of London (1666). In Malaysia, modern insurance stems from British regulation (Insurance Act 1963, FSA 2013), alongside Takaful introduced in 1984 under BNM Shariah oversight.

1.2 Risk & Risk Management

Definition of Risk

Uncertainty regarding financial loss or exposure to peril, hazard, and harm.

Classification of Risk

Risk Type Possible Outcome Example
Pure Risk Loss or no loss only Factory fire, road accident
Speculative Risk Profit, loss, or breakeven Stock market investment
Fundamental Risk Widespread impact Pandemic, war, inflation
Particular Risk Specific to an individual/group Personal theft, illness

1.3 Peril, Hazard, and Loss

  • Peril: The prime cause of a loss (e.g., Fire, Flood, Theft).
  • Hazard: A condition that increases the likelihood/severity of a loss (Physical, Moral, Morale, Legal).
  • Loss: The reduction or disappearance of economic value following a peril.

1.4 Risk Handling Methods

  1. Avoidance: Ceasing or avoiding hazardous activities.
  2. Prevention: Actions to lower loss probability.
  3. Mitigation: Controlling severity (e.g., sprinkler systems).
  4. Retention: Bearing minor losses internally (deductibles/excess).
  5. Transfer: Shifting risk to an insurer via an insurance contract.

Chapter 1 Review Questions

1. What is the correct definition of a pure risk?

Answer: A risk where there is a possibility of financial loss or no loss (no profit potential).

Chapter 2: Fundamental Principles of Insurance

2.1 The Six Universal Principles

  1. Utmost Good Faith (Uberrimae Fidei): Full, truthful disclosure of all material facts by both parties.
  2. Insurable Interest: Legal/financial relationship requiring financial loss if damage occurs.
  3. Indemnity: Restoring the insured to the exact financial position held prior to loss.
  4. Subrogation: Insurer’s right to step into the insured's shoes to recover payouts from negligent third parties.
  5. Contribution: Sharing loss proportionally among multiple insurers covering the same risk.
  6. Proximate Cause: The dominant/primary cause setting the chain of events into motion.

2.2 Life Insurance Application

Life insurance contracts are not indemnity contracts (predetermined sum assured), and subrogation/contribution generally do not apply. Insurable interest must exist at inception (FSA 2013 Schedule 8) for spouses, children, employees, or dependents.

Non-Contestability Provision (FSA 2013)

If a life policy remains in force for over 2 years during the insured's lifetime, the insurer cannot void the policy for misstatements unless proven intentionally fraudulent on a material matter.

2.3 General Insurance Application

General insurance operates strictly on Indemnity, Subrogation, and Contribution. Ongoing duty of disclosure exists when material changes occur (e.g., modifying property, adding drivers, or renewing policies).

Chapter 2 Review Questions

1. Which principle grants insurers the right to recover claim payments from responsible third parties?

Answer: Subrogation.
