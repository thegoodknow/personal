Chapter 1: Nature & Structure of Investment-Linked Insurance (ILP)

Investment-linked life insurance policies combine insurance protection with investment in unitized funds managed by professional managers. It was introduced in Malaysia in December 1999.

Core Differences: Traditional vs. Investment-Linked Policies

Feature Traditional Insurance Investment-Linked Policy (ILP)
Investment Risk Borne by Insurance Company Borne entirely by Policyholder
Cash Value Guarantee Guaranteed cash surrender values Non-guaranteed (depends on unit performance)
Structure Bundled (Protection & savings integrated) Unbundled (Protection charges and investments separated)
Flexibility Fixed premium payment schedules Flexible premium top-ups, switches, & premium holidays

Individual Income Tax Relief Limits

  • Life Insurance & EPF Relief: Up to RM7,000 total (Max RM3,000 for Life Insurance premiums, Max RM4,000 for EPF).
  • Medical & Education Insurance: Up to RM3,000.
  • Private Retirement Scheme (PRS) & Deferred Annuity: Up to RM3,000.

Chapter 2: Types of Funds & Investment Risk Profiles

Investment-linked premiums purchase units in specific funds selected by the policyholder based on their personal risk tolerance.

Major Investment-Linked Fund Types

  • Equity Funds: Invest primarily in stocks/shares. Highest long-term capital growth potential, but highest volatility/risk.
  • Fixed Income / Bond Funds: Invest in government securities and corporate bonds. Provides steady income stream with moderate risk.
  • Money Market Funds: Invest in short-term liquid debt instruments (e.g., treasury bills, bank deposits). Capital preservation focus with lowest risk.
  • Managed / Balanced Funds: Invest in a blend of equities, fixed income, and cash to balance risk and growth.

Key Investment Risks

  • Market Risk: Value of underlying investments fluctuates due to macroeconomic factor movements.
  • Interest Rate Risk: Increases in interest rates lead to drops in fixed-income bond prices.
  • Credit/Default Risk: The issuer of a bond defaults on interest or principal repayments.
  • Liquidity Risk: Inability to sell assets quickly without taking a heavy loss.
  • Dollar-Cost Averaging (DCA): The practice of investing equal amounts at regular intervals regardless of unit price. You buy more units when prices fall and fewer when prices rise, smoothing out volatility over time.

Chapter 3: Rules, Allocation Rates & Policy Charges

Key Policy Charges

  • Fund Management Charge (FMC): Capped at 1.0% p.a. of NAV for Money Market/Fixed Income funds, and 1.5% p.a. of NAV for Equity/Balanced funds. Deduction is built into the daily NAV calculation.
  • Cost of Insurance (COI): Charges deducted monthly by cancelling units to pay for mortality and morbidity coverage. COI increases as the life assured gets older.
  • Policy / Administration Fee: Fixed monthly charge deducted via unit cancellation.
  • Switching Fee: Fee charged when moving investments between funds (many insurers allow 1–2 free switches yearly).

Chapter 4: Disclosures & Policy Operations

  • Free-Look Period: 15 calendar days starting from the date the policy is delivered. Cancellation within this period entitles the policyholder to a full premium refund minus medical expenses.
  • Single Pricing vs Dual Pricing: Dual pricing (bid/offer 5% spread) was phased out; Single Pricing (NAV-based) has been mandatory since 2006 to increase transparency.
  • Product Illustrations: Must project returns under 2 rates: Scenario X (2%) and Scenario Y (5% or historical index).
  • Annual Statement: Issued to policyholders within 2 months after financial year-end, details unit balance, charge deductions, and policy sustainability updates.
  • Unit-Deducting Riders (UDR): Optional supplementary riders whose charges are paid by deducting units from the policy fund value without requiring separate regular premiums.
